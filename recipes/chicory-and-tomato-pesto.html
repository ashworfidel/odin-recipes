Chicory and tomato pesto

Description:

Pesto designates a kind of sauce made by mashing ingredients to a cream, most often used with pasta. This version is made of chicory and tomato, but the most known is pesto genovese. Do not confuse them: Italian people are very sensitive about food!

  • Preparation Time:
    • ⏲️ Prep time: 30 min
    • 🍳 Cook time: 0 min
    • 🍽️ Servings: 4-6

Ingredients

Quantities may vary a lot: it depends on your taste and ingredients flavours. You will most probably need to adapt them.

  • ~50 g cleaned chicory leaves. You should pick young and tender ones, which are more juicy and less bitter.
  • ~30 g fresh tomato
  • ~4 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil
  • ~80 g aged and savoury cheese
  • ~25 g almonds
  • 2 cloves garlic
  • coarse-grained salt

Steps

  1. Remove the most watery part of the tomato pulp. You don’t need a perfect work, but the drier, the better. Place garlic in a mortar and mash it to a cream with a pestel.
  2. Add almonds and grind them. They must be reduced to small pieces, not powdered. You won’t be able to crumble them further after this step.
  3. Add chicory, cleaned tomatoes and very little coarse-grained salt and mash to a cream. The main function of the salt is to help mashing: make sure it disappears. The result will be more or less liquid depending on the vegetables you use, but don’t worry about it yet.
  4. Grate the cheese, then add into the mortar and mash to a cream. The result should be pretty uniform and firm, except some floating fibers. If it is too liquid add some cheese.
  5. Add 2 tablespoons oil and mash until uniform.
  6. If you are not satisfied with the texture, add 1 more tablespoon oil and mash again. Repeat until you like it, but remember that the result must be a uniform cream, without visible oil in it.
  7. You can now use pesto, or store it in a jar in the fridge for 2-3 days. It won’t last long because it is just raw mashed vegetables and oil.
